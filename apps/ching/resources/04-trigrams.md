# The eight trigrams

Every hexagram is built from two smaller figures of three lines each, called trigrams. There are exactly eight of them, because three lines that can each be solid or broken give 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 combinations. Learn the eight trigrams and you can read the structure of every hexagram at a glance.

## The eight

| Trigram | Name | Image | Quality | Family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ☰ | Qián 乾 | Heaven | Strong, creative | Father |
| ☷ | Kūn 坤 | Earth | Yielding, receptive | Mother |
| ☳ | Zhèn 震 | Thunder | Arousing, moving | Eldest son |
| ☵ | Kǎn 坎 | Water | Deep, dangerous | Middle son |
| ☶ | Gèn 艮 | Mountain | Still, resting | Youngest son |
| ☴ | Xùn 巽 | Wind | Gentle, penetrating | Eldest daughter |
| ☲ | Lí 離 | Fire | Clinging, bright | Middle daughter |
| ☱ | Duì 兌 | Lake | Joyous, open | Youngest daughter |

Read each trigram from the bottom up, just like a hexagram.

## How to see them

The images aren't arbitrary. Look at the lines:

- **Heaven** is all solid: pure strength. **Earth** is all broken: pure openness.
- **Thunder** has one solid line at the bottom pushing up under two open ones: energy breaking out from below.
- **Mountain** has one solid line on top, resting over two open ones: something firm and still, settled above.
- **Water** has a solid line hidden in the middle, between two open lines: strength surrounded by danger, like a current in a ravine.
- **Fire** has an open line in the middle, between two solid ones: bright on the outside, empty at the center, like a flame that needs something to cling to.
- **Wind** has one open line at the bottom under two solid ones: something soft that slips in underneath and penetrates.
- **Lake** has one open line on top over two solid ones: a calm, open surface over depth.

## The family

Tradition pictures the eight as a family. Heaven and Earth are the parents. The other six are their children, and the rule is simple: a trigram takes its gender from its one line that differs from the other two. One solid line among broken ones makes a son; one broken line among solid ones makes a daughter. The position of that line gives the birth order: at the bottom, the eldest; in the middle, the middle child; on top, the youngest.

This is an old cosmological picture, not a statement about how families or genders should be. It's useful as a memory aid, and it shows how the system thinks: in relationships, not isolated things.

## How trigrams make a hexagram

A hexagram is one trigram placed on top of another. The lower trigram (lines 1 to 3) is called the inner trigram, and it often describes what's happening within you or at the root of the situation. The upper trigram (lines 4 to 6) is the outer trigram, describing what's around you or what's emerging.

Take Hexagram 3, Beginning: Water over Thunder. Thunder below is energy stirring; Water above is danger and difficulty. Put them together and you get the hexagram's picture: new life pushing up into a dangerous, tangled situation. Every Image text is built exactly this way, by reading the relationship between the two trigrams.

Eight trigrams below, eight above: 8 × 8 = 64. That's why there are 64 hexagrams, no more and no fewer.

## Two arrangements

You'll often see the trigrams arranged in a circle. There are two traditional circles. The "Earlier Heaven" arrangement, attributed to Fu Xi, sets opposites facing each other: Heaven across from Earth, Fire across from Water. The "Later Heaven" arrangement, attributed to King Wen, follows the seasons and the compass. The second is the one used in feng shui and in the directions the I Ching text sometimes mentions, such as "friends in the southwest."
